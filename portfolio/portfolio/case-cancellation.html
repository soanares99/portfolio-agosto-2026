Same-Day Loan Cancellation

How I audited and redesigned PicPay's Personal Loan cancellation flow to support same-day (D0) cancellation — fixing confusing content, clarifying what gets debited, and defining the tracking and research plan behind it.

01

Overview

Letting people undo a loan the same day they took it

PicPay's Personal Loan already let customers cancel a contract, but the existing flow only allowed cancellation between one and seven days after contracting (D+1 to D+7). The business wanted to extend that to the same day the loan was taken out (D0) — with a simpler rule: the customer only needs to return the amount they received, with IOF and interest waived. I audited the flow that had been built for this, fixed the content and logic gaps I found, and designed the "desired" version of the journey along with the metrics needed to validate it.

02

The problem

A flow that raised more questions than it answered

Reviewing the journey that had already been drafted, I found several inconsistencies between what the content promised and what the product actually did — the kind of gaps that quietly drive customers to Support.

Issues found
  • IOF messaging contradicted itself: one screen said it's charged "even if you cancel," another showed "IOF: R$ 0.00"
  • The insurance line promised a refund that PicPay doesn't control — it's between the insurer and the company
  • Nowhere did the copy explicitly say the amount would be debited from the customer's balance
  • The confirmation button read a generic "Continue," giving no signal that this step confirmed the cancellation
  • Balance was checked only after the customer tried to confirm — not before
  • The end of the flow had no visible feedback, leaving customers unsure if the cancellation actually went through
Resolution principles
  • Say explicitly what will happen — debit, refund, and each fee — before the customer confirms
  • Never promise something PicPay doesn't control, like an insurer's refund timeline
  • Buttons say what they do: "Confirm cancellation," not "Continue"
  • Move blocking checks (like balance) as early as possible in the flow
  • Always close the loop with visible, explicit feedback at the end
03

The process

From a patched-up flow to the version I'd actually ship

Phase 1 Same-day cancellation (D0), adjusted

The first priority was shipping same-day cancellation with the content problems fixed. Unlike the D+1 to D+7 flow already in production, D0 only requires the customer to return what they received, with IOF and interest waived; insurance is handled directly with the insurer, and any registration fee charged is refunded.

Explicit debit language

Rewrote the value breakdown so the copy states plainly that the amount will be debited from the customer's PicPay balance — removing the ambiguity that led people to expect an extra confirmation step.

Clear confirmation action

Replaced the generic "Continue" with "Confirm cancellation" so the button itself signals that tapping it finalizes the request.

Full tracking definition

Defined the event list for the flow — screen views and button clicks — and registered every event in PicPay's internal event catalog, alongside accessibility specs for screen readers.

Phase 2 The desired journey

With Phase 1 fixing what was broken, the desired journey is the version built around what customers actually need — starting with moving the balance check to before the confirmation step, not after.

Balance check moved earlier

Instead of letting someone confirm and then finding out they lack the balance, the check happens before they ever reach the confirmation screen — with a direct path to add funds if needed.

Inline motivation survey

A one-question survey ("What's the main reason for canceling?") shown right before confirmation, capturing intent at the moment it's strongest — without redirecting outside the app.

Contextual loading & richer feedback

Generic loading states were replaced with contextual messaging while the system validates balance and processes the cancellation, followed by a feedback screen with an NPS prompt and a clear "what happens next."

Design decision: rather than waiting for every piece of the desired journey to be ready, Phase 1 ships the content fixes immediately while Phase 2 layers in the structural changes — so customers stop hitting confusing copy long before the full journey is rebuilt.

Metrics & instrumentation

Defining how we'll know it's working

Alongside the flow, I defined the cancellation funnel, the events behind it, and the goals we'd track once it ships — plus a segmentation plan to understand who's canceling and why.

Target goals
  • Completion rate above 70% (of users who enter with sufficient balance)
  • Balance-block rate below 25%, by catching it earlier in the flow
  • Block-resolution rate above 40% (add funds or link Open Finance)
  • Support call volume down 30%+ within 30 days of launch
  • Inline NPS response rate above 20%
Segmentation plan
  • By registration fee charged — do customers with a fee cancel more or less?
  • By entry point — where cancellations originate in the app
  • By platform — iOS vs. Android behavior differences
  • By loan value range — does ticket size affect cancellation rate?
05

Next steps

What comes after the content fixes ship

In progress

With Phase 1 ready to ship, the next step is building the motivation and post-cancellation NPS surveys through PicPay's internal survey tool, then rolling out the desired journey — moving the balance check earlier and adding the transparent insufficient-balance screen — and validating the funnel against the goals defined above.
