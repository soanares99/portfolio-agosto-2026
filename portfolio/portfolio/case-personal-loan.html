Overview

The product context

"Personal Loan" is one of the most well-known loan types — it lets people borrow money and pay it back monthly with interest. At PicPay, I was the Product Designer responsible for conceiving the new personal loan application experience. Our main goal was to raise the conversion rate, making sure users completed the application process end-to-end — significantly increasing revenue.

02

The problem

A journey full of unnecessary friction

An initial analysis showed that the existing journey presented and requested many things without purpose — they delivered no benefit to customers or the business, only added friction and lengthened the process.

Pain points identified
  • Steps requested with no purpose for the customer or the business
  • Outdated interface, built on an old version of the company's Design System
  • No business definitions to understand what actually mattered
  • Inconsistency with PicPay's other loan products
  • Lack of documentation for the journey under analysis
  • Cognitive overload from too many screens and decisions
Design principles that guided the solution
  • Standardization with PicPay's other loan products
  • Show only what is relevant to people
  • Accessible for people with visual impairments
  • Reduce cognitive load at every step
  • Consistency and cohesion across the whole flow
  • A simpler, faster path to getting the money

The process

Streamlining from discovery to delivery

Phase 1 Understanding the existing journey

To get an accurate view of the scope, we involved the tech team to access a test environment showing every possible scenario. We also set up weekly 30-minute alignment meetings with the tech and product teams.

Every scenario mapped

We accessed a test environment to visualize every possible path of the existing journey — not just the happy path.

Weekly alignment

30-minute meetings with the tech and product teams ensured continuous alignment from the very start of the process.

Business definitions

Closing the gap in documentation and business definitions was essential to understanding what mattered to the company.

Phase 2 Defining the full scope

The new journey went far beyond the screens. We considered the whole system of touchpoints around the application experience.

Entry points Email & push notifications Application journey Tracking definition Validation feedback Accessibility (visual impairment)
Phase 3 A collaborative simulation screen

This step was highly collaborative — we needed to define a simulation screen that worked for six loan products, with some variations but strong consistency. Simulation information is shown on a single screen, letting people edit the data however they want and see how their changes reflect on the final offer in real time.

Phase 4 Working within constraints

Due to time and resource constraints, the tech team said it wouldn't be feasible to build contextual feedback for the end of the application (approved, denied, or under review). We designed a generic feedback state that applied to every scenario — the final answer would be delivered via push notification and email.

Design decision: instead of blocking delivery while waiting for a perfect contextual feedback, we designed a generic final state that worked across every scenario and paired it with robust push/email communication — keeping the journey lean and shippable on time.

Results

A full rollout — and measurable impact

By product decision, the whole journey was shipped to production at once — meaning the entire existing journey was replaced by the new one in a single release. After that deploy, we closely monitored the user experience to suggest new research and improvements.

On launch
50%

Conversion increase measured and reported by the Business team right after the new journey went live.

Over time
38%

Over time, the improvement settled at 38% — a lasting conversion gain, reflecting the long-term impact of the redesign.

Next step

Since there was residual improvement still to implement, it was important to keep it in mind and bring it up in following discussions — making sure it was prioritized and implemented in the next cycle.
