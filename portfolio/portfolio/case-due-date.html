Overview

One button, many possible states behind it

"Change due date" sounds like a simple feature — pick a new day, done. In practice, a Personal Loan customer can be current, overdue by one or several installments, mid-payment on a partial installment, or already refinanced (extended or with cash-out) — and each state changes whether the change is even allowed. I designed the eligibility logic, the date-selection experience, and the content that explains what changes, plus the research plan to validate comprehension before this ships across every loan product.

02

The challenge

Complexity that had to stay invisible to the customer

The eligibility logic behind this feature is genuinely complex — but the customer should never feel that. Whatever state their loan is in, they need a clear answer: yes you can, here's how; or no you can't, here's why and what to do instead.

What made it hard
  • The feature needs to work across five loan products with different underlying rules
  • Eligibility depends on loan status: current, due today, overdue by one installment, overdue by two or more, partially paid, or already refinanced
  • A refinanced loan behaves differently depending on whether it was extended or had cash-out — extended contracts can't be extended again, but can still get cash-out
  • Changing the due date recalculates the installment value, IOF, interest, and the contract's end date — all of which needed to be shown transparently
  • The change can only be made once per loan — a rule easy to state, easy to miss
Principles behind the solution
  • Offer pre-calculated quick date options first, with a full calendar as a fallback
  • Always show a clear before/after: old value and date next to the new ones
  • Block ineligible states early, with a plain-language reason and a next step — pay, refinance, or go home
  • Reuse PicPay's existing loan-management components so the feature feels native, not bolted on
03

The process

From decision tree to screen content

Phase 1 Mapping the eligibility logic

Before designing a single screen, I mapped every loan state that could reach this feature — because the entry point is the same button, but what happens after it depends entirely on the account behind it.

Current, no pending installments Installment due today Overdue — 1 installment Overdue — 2+ installments Partially paid installment Refinanced — extended Refinanced — cash-out
Phase 2 Designing the date-selection flow

For eligible customers, I designed three pre-calculated quick options — longer, medium, and shorter grace periods — each showing the updated installment value up front, plus a "see all available dates" option that opens a full calendar for anyone who wants more control.

Quick options first

Three ready-made dates calculated from the loan's grace-period parameters, each labeled with its updated installment value — no math required from the customer.

Calendar as fallback

A full calendar view opens with no date pre-selected, showing the month with the last possible choice — the "Continue" button only enables once a date is picked.

Same-day nuance

If an installment is due that same day, the copy makes clear it won't be affected — the change only applies to future installments, avoiding any confusion about what's being charged right now.

Phase 3 Making the trade-off transparent

Moving the due date isn't free — it adds days to the loan, which recalculates the installment value, IOF, interest, and the contract's end date. I designed a dedicated comparison screen so none of that is a surprise.

Side-by-side comparison

Old due date and value shown directly against the new ones, with IOF, interest, and the new contract end date broken out — plus a link explaining why the values change.

One-time-only disclaimer

A persistent note that this change can only be made once per loan, placed right above the confirm button — not buried in a paragraph above it.

Phase 4 Designing for the edge cases

Customers with overdue installments can't change their due date — but simply hiding the option would be confusing. I designed dedicated blocking screens that explain why and offer a real next step.

Clear blocking message

"Keep your installments up to date to unlock this" replaces a dead-end error, paired with the specific reason: an overdue or partially paid installment.

A real path forward

Instead of a dead end, the customer sees direct actions — pay the overdue installment, or refinance for extra breathing room — depending on what they're eligible for.

Multi-channel confirmation

Once a change is processed, the customer gets in-app feedback plus a push notification and email confirming the new due date — closing the loop the same way the cancellation flow does.

04

Usability research plan

What we need to validate before this rolls out everywhere

Because this feature touches five products with different rules, I defined a set of moderated-research questions to run before expanding past the first product — testing discoverability, comprehension, and behavior, not just whether people can technically complete the flow.

Discoverability Is the "Change due date" button easy to find from the loan management screen?
Comprehension Do people understand the updated-value text next to each quick date option?
Behavior Do people pick one of the ready-made dates, or go straight to the full calendar?
Comprehension Do people understand the left column of the comparison table refers to their current due date?
Comprehension Do people notice and understand the "only once per loan" disclaimer before confirming?
Flow timing Does placing the satisfaction survey right after entering a password create anxiety before people see confirmation feedback?
Content clarity Is the overall content of the flow understandable end to end, without needing Support?

Next steps

In progress

The plan is to run moderated usability sessions against the questions above on the Clean loan product first, since it has the simplest eligibility rules, then extend the feature to Refin, Salary-linked, CredFlex, and PicPay Parcela once the core flow is validated.
